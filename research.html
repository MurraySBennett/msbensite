Research

How do people combine information, recognise patterns and make decisions when they are unsure? I study these questions with behavioural experiments and mathematical models.

I work in three main areas. In human-AI collaboration, I study what it costs a person to coordinate with a teammate, when human-AI collaboration is efficient, what information helps people work with AI partners, and how AI recommendations can be improved so that human collaborators perform better. With mathematical models of cognition, I aim to account for how people process information, particularly in dynamic and uncertain environments where the correct response, or good performance over time, is not always clear. In perception, I study how people judge visual features in complex, real-world tasks such as melanoma identification, and I develop reliable statistical tools that help people build expertise quickly.
