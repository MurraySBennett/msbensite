# Fantasy lineup pilot: backtest evidence and release decision

**Decision, 2026-09-30:** Keep `/tools/fantasy-lineup/` an unlisted, noindex experiment. There are **zero admissible historical weeks** with archived issue-time inputs and reviewed final outcomes in this repository. The generative model has not beaten the rolling baseline on real held-out weeks, its 80% intervals and event probabilities are uncalibrated, and the author-run scenario comprehension check has not occurred. No superiority or general human–model benefit is established.

## Reproduce the current evidence check

From the repository root, run:

```sh
python3 -m fantasy.backtest --seasons 2023 2024 2025 --seed 7026
python3 -m unittest tests.test_fantasy_backtest -v
```

The first command reads no catalog because none has been approved. It reports `weeks=0`, `real_weeks=0`, `paired_players=0`, `synthetic_rosters=0`, `calibration.n=0`, `calibration.bins=null`, and `release_decision="insufficient real evidence"`. No historical cutoff or source hash can be cited for those seasons. The second command exercises the evaluation machinery on deliberately synthetic, checksum-backed fixtures; passing tests establish code behavior, not predictive validity.

The fixture case is 2026 week 5, cutoff **2026-10-02 18:00 UTC**, with invented capture times at 16:00 UTC, seed 4, 24 draws and four requested synthetic rosters. Its small player pool yields only **one distinct roster**. It has four forecast players, three paired with the last-three-score baseline, one player without history excluded from the paired comparison, and one simulated game. The paired positions are WR (2) and K (1); QB, RB and TE have zero paired observations. On those three invented outcomes, model mean absolute point error is **8.921**, baseline **7.033**; model 80% interval coverage is **2/3**, baseline empirical-interval coverage **0/3**; Brier scores for at least 10 PPR points are **0.112** and **0.333** respectively. The one roster produces one model/baseline disagreement in one week; oracle regret is **0** for the model and **6** for the baseline. These numbers are fixture diagnostics only. The model has worse fixture point error, and the toy roster cannot rescue that result or support a policy claim.

Fixture ablations remove recent target/carry/attempt history and injury information one group at a time while holding the seed fixed. Removing recent usage increases paired fixture MAE by **1.879** points; removing injury information increases it by **1.772** points. Neither changes a fixture lineup. Opponent-position, past score/clock and weather groups lack admissible inputs and are **prospective only**. Season phase is known before kickoff but is **not modeled**. There are **zero** prospectively frozen real availability or usage observations, so scenario Brier score and usage coverage are unavailable.

## Prespecified comparison when evidence arrives

`run_backtest(seasons, cutoff_policy, seed)` requires a **distinct archive directory per historical cutoff**, with a dated capture catalog and four checksum-verified nflverse CSV assets. It rejects source captures, injury updates, games and feature records after the cutoff, current-week stat rows, observed game weather or score passed as a pregame feature, and incomplete or invalid final outcome rows. A historical case also requires the approved nflverse final-stat URL, CC BY 4.0 licence, checksummed raw and normalized outcome files, and reviewer/time fields; the normalized file must match every scored row. Human review must still verify that a downloaded source is authentic and correctly normalized—metadata and hashes alone cannot prove that. Today's release files cannot be assigned a past retrieval time. The current injury release lacks `date_modified`; a later download cannot reconstruct an earlier injury report.

Weekly stat exports can omit an active-roster player who recorded no stat. The harness never turns an absent row into zero: a reviewer must verify and record an explicit zero row or exclude that week. This normalization step has no reviewed real example yet.

The [nflverse-data repository licence](https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data/blob/main/LICENSE.md) declares CC BY 4.0, while its [injury dictionary](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/articles/dictionary_injuries.html) documents `date_modified`. The exact 2026 asset inspected for this pilot lacked that field; source and upstream rights still need review before any real-data publication.

Both methods use the same PPR coefficients and eligible players. The model's mean and 10th–90th percentile come from seeded joint draws; the baseline mean and empirical range use up to three earlier player scores. The baseline has no estimate for a player with zero prior games, so such players are counted as model-only and excluded from *paired* error and roster comparisons; their model error and coverage are reported separately. Report point MAE, 80% interval coverage and Brier score for the prespecified event of at least 10 PPR points overall, by QB/RB/WR/TE/K and by zero, 1–2, or at least 3 prior games. Calibration bins appear only with at least 20 paired predictions and at least five per bin. A player with only one earlier score has a degenerate baseline interval; coverage from that interval is weak evidence.

The seeded roster sampler chooses distinct player sets from the pregame common pool without consulting outcomes. When possible it samples two QBs, two RBs, two WRs, one TE and one K for QB/RB/WR/TE/K/FLEX/SUPERFLEX slots; the tiny fixture uses two WRs and one K for WR/K slots. For every sampled roster, both policies face identical legal choices and the same realized hindsight oracle. These are **synthetic rosters, never actual league rosters**. Report sample, disagreement and disagreement-week counts; fewer than eight independent disagreement weeks cannot support a policy claim, and multiple rosters from one week are dependent observations. The fixture sampler does not test a real superflex week; that gate remains open.

Feature ablations are individual comparisons against the full model using the same seed and outcome pool. Recent opportunity history and an archived issue-time injury report can be ablated; other proposed groups wait for pre-kickoff historical inputs and an implemented feature. Observed game weather and final game state never enter a forecast. Fixture scenario checks require a hashed pregame snapshot whose source captures, seed and complete draw shards match the replayed forecast; only then are availability probabilities and conditional active usage ranges compared with later observations. **Real scenario observations are rejected** until a reviewed participation/usage source adapter exists; the current real-data denominator is zero. Fixture and historical catalogs cannot be pooled. Player-level calibration bins are descriptive, with uncertainty clustered by week still unresolved.

## Remaining release gates

1. Freeze authentic pre-kickoff inputs and reviewed final outcomes for enough rolling weeks, including QB, kicker, sparse-history and superflex cases. Report all cutoffs, source hashes, exclusions and failures.
2. Review model versus baseline error, coverage, calibration and regret on the identical eligible pools. A model that loses the baseline stays an experiment or is revised and retested on later weeks.
3. Run one short author-led comprehension check with another reader. Prompt: “If the card says *if this questionable player is active and has normal usage*, is it saying the player will play, or showing what the lineup would be under that assumption? Does pressing What if? change the saved official advice?” Count correct responses in aggregate only; keep no individual answer or identity in the repository. **Not yet run.**
4. Review a real end-to-end weekly update, source attribution, stale handling, legal lineup and corrected result in the built site. Until these gates pass, keep the Tools-page link and sitemap entry absent and the route noindex.
