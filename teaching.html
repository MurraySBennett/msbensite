Teaching Philosophy

The thing students most need from a quantitative psychology course is not a procedure to follow — it's a feeling for why the procedure works, and a sense of what breaks when the assumptions don't hold. That intuition is hard to transmit through lecture alone. My approach is to put students in contact with data as quickly as possible, so they can develop that intuition by doing rather than watching.

In practice this means running live experiments during class, having students analyse their own responses, and explicitly connecting what the model says to what they actually experienced as a participant. The experiment demos on this site grew directly out of that approach — they're the same paradigms I've used in teaching, made available so anyone can try them and see what a decision-making task feels like from the inside.

I'm particularly interested in making the mathematics of cognitive science accessible to students who don't come in with a strong quantitative background. Bayesian reasoning, evidence accumulation, signal detection — these ideas are genuinely comprehensible if you encounter them through concrete examples first and formalism second. I hold Associate-level teaching accreditation through CIRTL (Centre for the Integration of Research, Teaching and Learning), completed in 2024.

I mentor undergraduate and graduate students in experimental design, programming, and data analysis — and I'm particularly attentive to the moment when someone hits a wall with quantitative material. Getting students past that wall, with their confidence intact, is one of the more satisfying things about this job.

Courses I'm Prepared to Teach

I can contribute to curricula across the following areas, from introductory to advanced level:

  • Cognitive psychology (perception, attention, memory, reasoning, decision making)
  • Research methods and experimental design
  • Frequentist and Bayesian statistics
  • Mathematical psychology and cognitive modelling
  • Machine learning methods and applications in psychology
  • Human-AI interaction and collaboration
  • Introduction to programming for psychological research (Python, MATLAB)

I'm particularly interested in developing courses that build a bridge between classical cognitive psychology and the computational methods that are reshaping the field — accessible to students without a strong mathematics background, but rigorous enough to be genuinely useful.
