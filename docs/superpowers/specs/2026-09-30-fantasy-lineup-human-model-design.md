# Fantasy lineup human–model tool — design

## Purpose and first user

The first user is Murray, making a real weekly NFL fantasy lineup decision in a Sleeper PPR league with a superflex slot and a kicker. The tool must recommend a usable lineup from Murray's available players and measure whether unaided human judgment, a statistical model, or the final human–model choice performs better. It must work without importing private league data. If the pilot proves usable, the same manual-entry flow can serve other leagues for free.

Success means Murray can enter a roster and exact league rules once, finish the weekly decision in a few minutes, see a legal recommendation with uncertainty before games lock, and return afterward to see how the three choices scored. Scientific success is narrower: honest, prospective comparisons and calibrated model predictions, not a claim that psychology improved performance after one season.

## Decision workflow

1. **Set up once.** Enter rostered players, eligible positions, starting-slot counts, and the league's scoring values. The initial supported modeled positions are QB, RB, WR, TE and K; FLEX and SUPERFLEX accept their configured eligible positions. An unsupported slot such as D/ST is shown as user-fixed and is excluded from optimized-score comparisons. Confirm the league's nonstandard scoring values rather than silently assuming Sleeper defaults. Save configuration in this browser, with an export/import file for portability. The tool makes no Sleeper API call.
2. **Choose before advice.** For the current week, show the roster, opponents, game times and bye weeks without projections. Murray selects an unaided lineup and, for up to two uncertain slot decisions, gives a probability that the chosen player outscores the alternative. This step is optional for ordinary visitors, but required for a human–model comparison. A skip records no human baseline.
3. **Reveal advice.** Freeze the model's pre-game prediction and its input-data timestamp. Show its highest-expected-points legal lineup, point ranges, the nearest viable swap, and the data limitations most relevant to that swap. The model cannot claim to know breaking injury, lineup or weather information it has not received. Users can mark a player unavailable or questionable; the recommendation recomputes and records the changed assumption.
4. **Make the final call.** The user saves a final lineup and a short reason for each override. The display distinguishes model-only, unaided and final choices. A pick made or revised after a player's game has started is marked late and excluded from the prospective comparison for that player.
5. **Review results.** Once final player statistics arrive, score the modeled slots in all three saved lineups under the user's rules; label any user-fixed unsupported slot as excluded from these totals. Show realized points, the difference from the best legal modeled-slot lineup among the same pre-lock roster, whether each override helped or hurt, and the model's prediction accuracy. Keep prior weeks visible. Do not present one good or bad week as evidence of a general effect.

## Statistical model and psychological question

The player model predicts distributions of weekly fantasy points from information known before kickoff: prior weekly production, opportunity and usage, position, opponent, home/away, rest and bye information where reliably available. Use shrinkage for sparse histories and explicitly wide uncertainty for new or newly promoted players. Model the scoring components needed for the configured league, including kicking. Its first benchmark is a transparent rolling-history projection under the same scoring rules. Recommend the lineup with greatest expected points, not an implied probability of winning a league matchup; the latter would require the opponent's roster and a joint model.

The psychological contribution is **measured advice use under uncertainty**. The unaided choice and probability reveal the user's prior judgment; the final choice and reason reveal whether and when model advice changed it. The tool reports confidence calibration and the incremental value of overrides, alongside decision time if the user opts to record it. A later adaptive-reliance model may estimate when a person's information is complementary to the statistical model. No personalized weighting is learned from a handful of observations; until enough repeated decisions exist, show the three methods side by side.

Workload/fatigue, team morale and contract events are hypotheses for later feature tests, not assumed psychological mechanisms. A feature earns a place only if it is defined before evaluation, available before kickoff, and improves held-out predictions over the simpler model. Monte Carlo prediction of player outcomes is not, by itself, simulation-based inference. An SBI extension would need a simulator of latent advice-taking or player processes, inference from observations, and parameter-recovery/calibration checks that justify likelihood-free inference over a simpler fitted model.

## Data, architecture and privacy

Use [nflverse weekly player statistics](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/reference/load_player_stats.html), [schedules](https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data/releases/tag/schedules), and player identifiers to create a dated, versioned static data snapshot. The documented player-stat release includes offense and kicking. Attribute the sources and verify the licence for every shipped dataset. A reproducible offline Python update computes model outputs and a compact JSON snapshot; a static browser interface at `/tools/fantasy-lineup/` reads the snapshot. This fits the site's allowlisted build and avoids a server and user accounts. A failed or stale update leaves the last dated snapshot available with an obvious stale-data warning. No claims based on post-lock corrections enter a pre-game prediction.

Roster, scoring and decision records stay in browser storage unless the user exports a file. No analytics, private roster upload or third-party league API is needed for the first version. Exported records contain the entered roster, rules, picks, model outputs and timestamps; the browser must make that clear before export. A future optional Sleeper integration is a separate design and licence decision. The pilot route can be previewed without advertising an unvalidated tool on the public Tools page.

## Evaluation and release gates

- **Offline model test:** Rolling-origin backtests use only data available before each historical week. Compare point-prediction error, interval coverage and lineup regret with the rolling-history baseline, separately by position and sparse-history status. Include the kicker and superflex cases. Backtesting the model cannot manufacture historical unaided human choices.
- **Prospective human test:** Save the unaided, model and final recommendations before each game locks. Compare them on the identical eligible roster and scoring rules. Report paired weekly differences and uncertainty; with one user over one season, describe the result as a personal pilot, not population-level evidence or a proven cognitive mechanism.
- **Usability check:** Murray can create and revisit a week, spot stale data, correct a status assumption, and understand why a swap is recommended in a few minutes. A public visitor can skip the research step and still obtain a lineup.
- **Public release:** List it as a working tool only after the data pipeline, scoring rules, legal-slot constraints and results review work end to end; publish the backtest and limitations with it. A model that fails the simple baseline must be revised or presented as an experiment rather than a superior recommender.

## Boundaries and follow-on work

This first version does not import Sleeper rosters, optimize waiver claims or trades, predict league-matchup win probability, validate speculative mental-state proxies, or fit an SBI cognitive model without enough decisions. It does not cover an unsupported fantasy scoring rule by guessing. The next research step is to test whether pre-advice confidence and recorded reasons predict when a human override adds information, then compare a prespecified adaptive policy with human-only and model-only choices.
