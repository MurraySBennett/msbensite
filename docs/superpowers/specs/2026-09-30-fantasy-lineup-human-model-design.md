# Fantasy lineup human–model tool — design

## Purpose and first user

The first user is Murray, making a real weekly NFL fantasy lineup decision in a Sleeper PPR league with a superflex slot and a kicker. The tool must recommend a usable lineup from Murray's available players and measure whether unaided human judgment, a statistical model, or the final human–model choice performs better. It must work without importing private league data. If the pilot proves usable, the same manual-entry flow can serve other leagues for free.

Success means Murray can enter a roster and exact league rules once, finish the weekly decision in a few minutes, see a legal recommendation with uncertainty before games lock, and return afterward to see how the three choices scored. Scientific success is narrower: honest, prospective comparisons and calibrated model predictions, not a claim that psychology improved performance after one season.

## Decision workflow

1. **Set up once.** Enter rostered players, eligible positions, starting-slot counts, and the league's scoring values. The initial supported modeled positions are QB, RB, WR, TE and K; FLEX and SUPERFLEX accept their configured eligible positions. An unsupported slot such as D/ST is shown as user-fixed and is excluded from optimized-score comparisons. Confirm the league's nonstandard scoring values rather than silently assuming Sleeper defaults. Save configuration in this browser, with an export/import file for portability. The tool makes no Sleeper API call. Users can mark outside-roster players as pickup candidates, but must confirm they are available in their league before the tool calls one a possible pickup.
2. **Choose before advice.** For the current week, show the roster, opponents, game times and bye weeks without projections. A compact prior-game context view shows a team's score/win-probability trajectory and the player's opportunities by game state, so a close final score is not mistaken for a close contest throughout. These are descriptive observations, available to the person before the unaided choice, unless the predictive feature tests below justify using them in the model. Murray selects an unaided lineup and, for up to two uncertain slot decisions, gives a probability that the chosen player outscores the alternative. A checkbox records whether the user already saw another forecast or ranking; that choice remains useful but is labeled externally informed rather than unaided. This step is optional for ordinary visitors, but required for a human–model comparison. A skip records no human baseline.
3. **Reveal advice.** Freeze the model's pre-game prediction and its input-data timestamp. Show its highest-expected-points legal lineup, point ranges, the nearest viable swap, and the data limitations most relevant to that swap. Display injury report status and its update time, with a plain distinction between chance of playing and expected points if active. The model cannot claim to know breaking injury, lineup or weather information it has not received. Users can mark a player unavailable or questionable; the recommendation recomputes and records the changed assumption.
4. **Make the final call.** The user saves a final lineup and a short reason for each override. The display distinguishes model-only, unaided and final choices. A pick made or revised after a player's game has started is marked late and excluded from the prospective comparison for that player.
5. **Review results.** Once final player statistics arrive, score the modeled slots in all three saved lineups under the user's rules; label any user-fixed unsupported slot as excluded from these totals. Show realized points, the difference from the best legal modeled-slot lineup among the same pre-lock roster, whether each override helped or hurt, and the model's prediction accuracy. Keep prior weeks visible. Do not present one good or bad week as evidence of a general effect.

## Statistical model and psychological question

The offline update predicts a distribution for every fantasy-eligible QB, RB, WR, TE and K scheduled that week, keyed by player ID, game, model version and data cutoff. The browser then filters these common predictions to each person's roster and confirmed pickup candidates. The player model uses information known before kickoff: prior weekly production, opportunity and usage, position, opponent, home/away, rest and bye information where reliably available. Use shrinkage for sparse histories and explicitly wide uncertainty for new or newly promoted players. Model the scoring components needed for the configured league, including kicking. Its first benchmark is a transparent rolling-history projection under the same scoring rules. Recommend the lineup with greatest expected points, not an implied probability of winning a league matchup; the latter would require the opponent's roster and a joint model.

The [nflverse injury feed](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/articles/dictionary_injuries.html) includes listed injury type, practice participation, game status and modification time. A reported injury changes two uncertain quantities: chance of playing and likely points **if** the player plays. A player ruled out is ineligible; a questionable player has an inactive zero-point possibility and a conditional playing distribution. Injury type is retained as reported context, not treated as a severity scale; missing from the report does not prove health. Snapshot each status before lock because a retrospective injury table may contain later changes.

The week of season and forecast game-time weather can be tested as separate predictors, with roof status and byes accounted for. [National Weather Service hourly forecasts](https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-api) are open for US outdoor venues; international games or missing forecasts need an explicit unavailable state. A weather effect enters recommendations only after held-out or prospective tests show incremental value. Historical tests need a forecast that was actually available before the game, not observed weather or a later forecast substituted afterward. Season timing is a regularized trend, not automatic evidence of fatigue or motivation.

[nflverse play-by-play](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/articles/dictionary_pbp.html) records score, clock, modeled *game* win probability and player involvement. The model can therefore test pre-game summaries of **past** close versus lopsided play, late-game targets/carries and opponent defense against each position. Predefine and separately test recent-usage, opponent-position and score-and-clock feature groups. Keep a group only when held-out prediction and lineup choices improve over the simpler model. Historical garbage-time fantasy points still count; do not remove them merely because the real game's result was settled. If forecasting a future game script, integrate over a pre-game distribution rather than using that game's eventual score.

Score trajectories do not directly measure motivation, morale or psychological momentum; fewer late touches do not establish intentional resting. [Per-game snap counts](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/articles/dictionary_snap_counts.html) can inform usage, but [play-level participation is delayed until after the season for 2023 onward](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/articles/nflverse_data_schedule.html), so it cannot support a dependable current-week resting feature. Public play-by-play also does not identify which defender covered a specific receiver. The first version may use partially pooled opponent-team/position tendencies, but must not claim a named defender matchup or shadow coverage. A human reason such as “I expect this cornerback to shadow him” is recorded as a judgment to test, not silently converted into verified data.

The psychological contribution is **measured advice use under uncertainty**. The unaided choice and probability reveal the user's prior judgment; the final choice and reason reveal whether and when model advice changed it. The tool reports confidence calibration and the incremental value of overrides, alongside decision time if the user opts to record it. A later adaptive-reliance model may estimate when a person's information is complementary to the statistical model. No personalized weighting is learned from a handful of observations; until enough repeated decisions exist, show the three methods side by side.

Workload/fatigue, team morale and contract events are hypotheses for later feature tests, not assumed psychological mechanisms. A feature earns a place only if it is defined before evaluation, available before kickoff, and improves held-out predictions over the simpler model. Monte Carlo prediction of player outcomes is not, by itself, simulation-based inference. An SBI extension would need a simulator of latent advice-taking or player processes, inference from observations, and parameter-recovery/calibration checks that justify likelihood-free inference over a simpler fitted model.

## Data, architecture and privacy

Use [nflverse weekly player statistics](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/reference/load_player_stats.html), [schedules](https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data/releases/tag/schedules), pre-lock injury reports, and player identifiers to create a dated, versioned static data snapshot. The documented player-stat release includes offense and kicking. Attribute the sources and verify the licence for every shipped dataset. A reproducible offline Python update computes model outputs for the eligible player pool and a compact JSON snapshot; a static browser interface at `/tools/fantasy-lineup/` reads the snapshot. This fits the site's allowlisted build and avoids a server and user accounts. A failed or stale update leaves the last dated snapshot available with an obvious stale-data warning. No claims based on post-lock corrections enter a pre-game prediction.

Roster, scoring and decision records stay in browser storage unless the user exports a file. No analytics, private roster upload or third-party league API is needed for the first version. Exported records contain the entered roster, rules, picks, model outputs and timestamps; the browser must make that clear before export. A future optional Sleeper integration is a separate design and licence decision. The pilot route can be previewed without advertising an unvalidated tool on the public Tools page.

## Other people's forecasts

An outside forecast would make a valuable **separate expert benchmark**, and a later model-plus-expert blend might improve picks. Each imported projection would need a verified licence covering this use, a scoring definition, player identifiers, source-issued and retrieved timestamps, and a saved pre-lock snapshot. Compare the outside forecast, this model, and any blend on the same roster and scoring rules; learn blend weights only from earlier weeks. Position-only ranks cannot be added across QB, RB, WR and TE as if they were projected points in a superflex lineup.

No reviewed feed currently grants clear permission to retain and display its weekly projections in this public tool. [Boris Chen's tiers](https://www.borischen.co/2014/09/week-3-tiers.html) cluster FantasyPros expert rankings and are useful for a person to consult, but its public CSV is not a documented reuse licence. [FantasyPros documents a projections API](https://api.fantasypros.com/public/v2/docs), but its [standard terms](https://api.fantasypros.com/public/v2/terms-of-use) limit use to personal noncommercial purposes and prohibit competing products. Downloadable consensus rankings derived from it do not expand those rights. The first version therefore links to Boris Chen and other outside resources without copying their forecasts, and the user can record having consulted one. A third-party feed is added only after explicit terms cover retrieval, retention, analysis and public display. This boundary also keeps an outside service from silently controlling the core recommendation.

## Evaluation and release gates

- **Offline model test:** Rolling-origin backtests use only data available before each historical week. Compare point-prediction error, interval coverage and lineup regret with the rolling-history baseline, separately by position and sparse-history status. Test recent usage, opponent-position, score-and-clock, injury availability, forecast weather and season-phase feature groups individually before their combination; report whether each changes actual lineup picks, not just a fit statistic. Include the kicker and superflex cases. Never use realized current-week score, usage, coverage, weather or retrospective injury data in pre-game features. If issue-time weather or injury archives are absent, evaluate those features prospectively instead of pretending a retrospective test is valid. Backtesting the model cannot manufacture historical unaided human choices.
- **Prospective human test:** Save the unaided, model and final recommendations before each game locks. Compare them on the identical eligible roster and scoring rules. Report paired weekly differences and uncertainty; with one user over one season, describe the result as a personal pilot, not population-level evidence or a proven cognitive mechanism.
- **Usability check:** Murray can create and revisit a week, spot stale data, correct a status assumption, and understand why a swap is recommended in a few minutes. A public visitor can skip the research step and still obtain a lineup.
- **Public release:** List it as a working tool only after the data pipeline, scoring rules, legal-slot constraints and results review work end to end; publish the backtest and limitations with it. A model that fails the simple baseline must be revised or presented as an experiment rather than a superior recommender.

## Boundaries and follow-on work

This first version does not import Sleeper rosters, track a game live, optimize waiver claims or trades, predict league-matchup win probability, validate speculative mental-state proxies, or fit an SBI cognitive model without enough decisions. It does not cover an unsupported fantasy scoring rule by guessing. The next research step is to test whether pre-advice confidence and recorded reasons predict when a human override adds information, then compare a prespecified adaptive policy with human-only and model-only choices.
